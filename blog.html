Why the CPA (Concrete – Pictorial – Abstract) Approach Transforms How Students Learn Mathematics

Many students struggle with mathematics not because they lack ability, but because abstract symbols are introduced too early. Here is how the Singapore CPA model bridges the gap between everyday objects and algebraic thinking.

Throughout my years teaching Singapore IQ Mathematics on HOC247 Kids and working with secondary school learners, I have noticed a recurring pattern: when students memorize formulas without visualizing the underlying relationships, they quickly lose confidence when faced with unfamiliar word problems.

The CPA (Concrete – Pictorial – Abstract) framework solves this by guiding learners through three natural stages of understanding:

  • Concrete (Thực tiễn): Students interact with physical objects, measurement tools, or real-life scenarios (such as matchsticks, clock faces, or queueing arrangements).
  • Pictorial (Hình ảnh): Learners translate those hands-on experiences into visual bar models, diagrams, or geometric sketches that reveal patterns at a glance.
  • Abstract (Khái quát): Finally, students connect their visual models to formal mathematical notation, equations, and logical proofs with deep comprehension.

Whether I am teaching primary students in Vietnam or tutoring high school students in Sydney, starting with intuition and visual representation always makes mathematics more joyful and lasting.

3 Practical Habits for Excelling in HSC & IGCSE Mathematics Examinations

Preparing for high-stakes exams like the NSW HSC or Cambridge IGCSE requires more than solving past papers endlessly. Discover three structured study habits that help my tutoring students build accuracy and exam confidence.

In my one-on-one and small-group tutoring sessions in Sydney, students often ask how to turn their classroom knowledge into consistent exam marks. Here are the three core habits we practice every week:

  • 1. Build a Personalized "Error & Insight Log": Instead of simply checking the marking scheme and moving on, write down why an error happened (e.g., sign error, misreading domain restrictions, or skipping a justification step) and summarize the key takeaway in your own words.
  • 2. Master Structured Working Out: Both HSC and IGCSE examiners award method marks for clear mathematical communication. Setting out step-by-step reasoning not only secures partial marks but also reduces careless slips under time pressure.
  • 3. Interleaved Topic Revision: Rather than practicing 20 questions of the exact same type in a row, mix questions from Calculus, Trigonometry, and Probability so your brain practices identifying which strategy to use.

From Secondary Classrooms in Vietnam to UNSW Sydney: My Journey in Mathematics & STEM Education

Teaching across public, private, and international schools in Ho Chi Minh City and continuing my educational journey in Sydney has shaped my belief that great mathematics teaching transcends borders.

Looking back at our hands-on STEM projects at Binh Tri Dong Public Secondary School and Tan Phu High School and Secondary School, some of my favorite moments were watching students light up when they realized that geometry and algebra could help them design real physical models.

Moving to Sydney and being part of the vibrant academic community at UNSW, while tutoring local students and volunteering with community organizations like Vinnies and JNC, has broadened my perspective even further. Every classroom and culture has its own strengths, yet curiosity and encouragement remain the universal language of learning.
